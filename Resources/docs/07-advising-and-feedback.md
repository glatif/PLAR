# Part A: Existing workflow

## A.1 The advisor's role

- PLAR Advisors are the student's **most frequent point of contact**. They answer questions, review work, give suggestions and feedback, and "support students' efforts to demonstrate their learning to the best of their ability" [P1-B p.11; P2-I p.6].
- **They do not assess.** Feedback should guide the student "toward a stronger expression of learning – we are not assessing their learning" [P2-E "Tone of comments"]. The PLAR team "will not be able to comment on how many credits you may attain" [P2-I p.8].
- **They do not edit the student's writing.** They make a general note if there are errors or unclear writing [P2-I p.7].
- Assessors are told they can trust that the advisor has explained that **every checklist item is required**. Anything missing was the student's choice [P1-B p.11, p.118].

## A.3 Feedback rounds

- **First review (intensive).** Detailed feedback on every section, plus **two DoL competencies**: Communication and one of the student's choice [P2-I p.14; P2-B PDF p.15]. The P2-B thread shows this happening across several emails:
  - one for the CL, AR, IoL and EDL [PDF p.6–7]
  - one for the Communication and Information Gathering competencies [PDF p.9–12]
  - one for the remaining competencies and evidence [PDF p.4–5]
- **Second review (briefer).** Checks improvements and anything outstanding [P2-I p.14].
- **Final review.** Against the submission checklist [P2-A step 9; P2-E]. The advisor then confirms the portfolio is ready and asks the Manager to approve it [P2-B PDF p.2].
- **Timing guidance to students:** send 1–2 competencies early. DoL work that arrives late gets less detailed attention. Feedback can take several weeks [P2-I p.6–7; P2-B PDF p.15].
- **What advisors comment on** [P2-I p.7]:
  - formatting
  - organisation
  - how to show learning (experience vs learning, specific examples)
  - whether an example fits the competency
  - writing (a general note only)

## A.4 The form feedback takes

- **Word margin comments** in the student's file, especially the DoL. For example `Commented [KJ1]` … `[KJ12]` in [P2-C]. This is why students are asked to send drafts as Word documents [P2-I p.62].
- **A summary email for each round**, organised by section or competency, with practical tips and attached resources (Bloom's, Speaking to Learning, the AR example, submission guidelines) [P2-B PDF p.4–7, p.9–12].
- Advisors **reassure students** that many comments do not mean the work is poor: it is "our way of 'speaking' with you" [P2-B PDF p.11].
- Common feedback themes in the samples [P2-B; P2-C]:
  - speak to the *how* and the *why*
  - describe learning, not experience
  - choose your best examples using Bloom's
  - keep competencies to 3–4 pages (5 at most)
  - trim evidence to 16–24 pieces (30 at most), with an EDL entry for each
  - add the "why" to EDL captions
  - split AR formal education into credits applied and not applied
  - state the credit request in the CL
  - avoid double dipping
  - proofread

## A.5 Tone rules for comments

From [P2-E "Tone of comments"]:

- Encouraging, impartial and supportive. Mix positive and constructive comments.
- Frame feedback as **what the student could do more of**, not what is lacking.
- **Guide; don't assess.** Use "this works well" or "I'd encourage you to elaborate", **not** "this is strong" or "I like this".
- Start with **longer "teaching comments"**, then move to briefer ones, even just questions.
- **"Don't question bombard!"**
- Refer to the competency at hand so the feedback points the student towards it.
- For upper-level petitions, encourage more complex examples (Bloom's).

## A.6 The standard comments library [P2-D]

**What it is.** A Word document titled *"DoL Feedback Standard Responses (PLAR Advisors, please feel free to add to this!)"*. It is a shared, growing library of **279 bulleted comments and prompts in 14 sections**. (The earlier chat summary mentioned "about 280". The figure is not two.)

| Section | Entries |
|---|---|
| Evidence | 8 |
| Only state learning which student hasn't gained credit for | 4 |
| Certificate learning discussed | 1 |
| Which examples to include & where (or pare down) | 25 |
| Speak To the Learning (Dig deeper) | 47 |
| MISC | 14 |
| Communication | 29 |
| Teamwork and Leadership | 23 |
| Information Gathering & Organization | 23 |
| Problem-Solving and Decision-Making | 19 |
| Numeracy | 16 |
| Critical & Creative Thinking | 29 |
| Intellectual Maturity and Independent Learning | 23 |
| Applied Knowledge and Skills | 18 |

**What it contains**
- Several alternative wordings, separated by "OR".
- **Placeholders** such as `XXXX`, `<<competency>>` and `_____`.
- Worked examples.
- For each competency, **"More probing questions ... (based on Details of Competencies)"**.
- Short conditional notes, such as "If student is keeping distant from anything in personal life, values etc.:".

**How it is used.** This is shown by example rather than described.
- Advisors paste entries **word for word** as Word comments. For instance:
  - P2-C comment KJ3 matches P2-D "Evidence" entry 1 word for word.
  - P2-C comment KJ2 contains the P2-D "Speak To the Learning" entry "It may be helpful to imagine that one of your team members..." word for word.
- Advisors then tailor the comment to the student.
- **Not documented:** how advisors search the library, how new entries are added or approved, and whether there are standard responses for sections other than the DoL.

**What this means for the proposed feature (B1).** The library is already organised by **competency** and **issue type**, which is how a suggestion engine would need to retrieve it. Entries are reused as written, and placeholders show where they get personalised.
