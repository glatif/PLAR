# Part A: Existing workflow

Assessors are **current or former TRU faculty, or subject-matter experts** [P2-I p.71 "Glossary"; P2-H p.2]. They are trained by:

- reading both handbooks and the student Moodle pages
- shadowing interviews
- doing a practice assessment, with feedback
- a first live assessment with extra preparation
- feedback from the Director and from mentors
- twice-yearly assessor meetings

## A.2 Sequence

```
Request received → (1) Independent pre-assessment, each assessor alone
                 → (2) Optional exchange of ratings and questions
                 → (3) Pre-interview meeting
                 → (4) Discussion of Learning interview
                 → (5) Post-interview consensus meeting
                 → (6) Writer drafts report → (7) Chair reviews → (8) Submit to PLAR
```

[P1-A steps 1–7; P1-B p.19–26 "Steps for Assessors"]. The handbook gives recommended lead times and durations for each step; these are left out of this documentation on purpose.

### Step 0: Request and receipt

- An assessor gets a request from an advisor to assess a portfolio [P1-A step 1].
- Both assessors **download the portfolio from Moodle**. They check that all files open and contact the PLAR Advisor if they don't, which happens about one time in five [P1-B p.19 "Step 1"].
- Questions about the portfolio go to the advisor who worked with the student [P1-B p.19].

### Step 1: Independent pre-assessment, part 1 (initial review for context)

- Each assessor works **alone**.
- They review the portfolio in any order, but thoroughly, keeping the "whole student" in view [P1-B p.19–20]. What they look at:
  - **AR**: chronology, durations, full- or part-time.
  - **IoL**: fields of expertise.
  - **DoL**: read front to back, noting questions and evidence.
  - **Evidence**: skimmed, using the EDL.
  - **Program Plan**: formal and transfer credits, to spot double dipping.
- They may search the web to verify claims [P1-B p.20, p.30, p.41].

### Step 1 (cont.): Independent pre-assessment, part 2 (detailed review and possible questions)

- **Re-read the DoL alongside each piece of evidence** and the CC2.0 rubric. Take notes on the rubric with specific examples [P1-B p.20–21].
- Give a **preliminary rating** to each competency: **I/S/O at both lower and upper level**. Finer informal grades (I+, S−, S+) are allowed [P1-B p.20].
- Draft **1–2 questions per competency** from the question bank [P1-D], aiming for a mix of conceptual and portfolio-specific, and retrospective and prospective. Criteria marked "yellow" (implied but not shown) make good questions [P1-B p.20, p.34, p.36–37].
- Form a **preliminary credit-award estimate** against what the student requested in the Program Plan [P1-B p.21].
- **Raise any integrity or authorship concern early** [P1-B p.23].
- Take a conservative stance: a rating can go up after the interview, but rarely down [P1-B p.21–22].

### Step 2: Exchange (recommended practice, not mandatory)

- Assessors are "strongly advised" to swap preliminary ratings, proposed questions and award estimates **before** the meeting [P1-B p.12–13, p.21–22].
- A common format is a **question-exchange document**, set out by competency. The first assessor sends questions and ratings; the second comments and adds their own [P1-B p.21].

### Step 3: Pre-interview meeting (both assessors)

- Share overall impressions, preliminary ratings and the award estimate. "Initial consensus is not expected" [P1-B p.22–23].
- Go through the questions to:
  - remove redundancy
  - cover both breadth and depth
  - pitch each question at the right level (LL or UL)
  - target gaps against the rubric ("what blanks need to be filled in?")

  [P1-B p.22]
- Finish with **one question per assessor per competency**: the chair's question and the writer's question, plus any held in reserve [P1-B p.23; P1-C "DoL Chair Question / DoL Writer Question"].
- Agree **roles (chair and writer)** [P1-A step 3] and the **ice-breaker** [P1-C header].
- The question bank is a suggestion list only. Assessors are expected to customise questions [P1-D introduction].

### Step 4: Discussion of Learning interview

**Setting**
- Zoom or teleconference; the student can choose video or audio only.
- A PLAR Advisor calls the student, connects the assessors, introduces everyone and **leaves**.
- If the call drops, the advisor reconnects it.

[P2-I p.67; P1-B p.121 "Interview Letter 2"]

**Purpose** [P1-B p.23 "Step 4"]
1. A chance for the student to reflect further on and expand the portfolio.
2. Check the work is authentic and free of integrity problems.
3. Let the student give extra evidence verbally.
4. Let the student show communication skill orally. This matters especially when their writing is weak or their background is oral, as is often the case for Indigenous students.

**Script** [P1-E]
1. Introductions, including the land acknowledgement ("traditional territory of ...") and short assessor bios.
2. The chair confirms the student's **program plan facts**: name, year, program, remaining credits, courses in progress, and LL/UL credits applied for.
3. Explanation of the format.
4. Student questions, then the **ice-breaker**.
5. The **8 competencies in order**. For each, the **writer asks first, then the chair**. There is a midway check on time.
6. Wrap-up: anything else to add; any last questions from the writer.
7. **Next steps** told to the student: the assessors confer, write the report, and send it to the Director. The report is emailed, and the credit details go to an advisor who updates the Program Plan.

**Conduct rules**
- Acknowledge answers **neutrally and affirmingly**. Never say "great answer", and don't hint at the outcome [P1-B p.37–38].
- Use probing questions sparingly. **Don't lead the student or put words in their mouth** [P1-B p.38, p.43].
- **Accommodate** ESL students and non-verbal or non-written communication [P1-B p.41–42].
- If the student is agitated: pause, remind them this is an advocacy interview, and offer to reschedule [P1-B p.38].
- Strong answers follow **STARL** [P1-B p.46].
- **No new evidence after the interview** unless the assessors ask for it through the Director [P1-B p.42; P1-B p.120 "Interview Letter 1"].
- A student gets only **one** Discussion of Learning [P2-I p.67].
- **Both assessors take notes**, often on the worksheet ("DoL Notes and Conclusions") [P1-B p.23; P1-C].

### Step 5: Post-interview consensus meeting

- Held **immediately after** the interview [P1-B p.24 "Step 5"].
- For each competency, each assessor gives a **revised rating and their reasons**:
  - If they agree, move on.
  - If not, compare notes against the rubric and reach consensus.
- Then agree the **credit-award recommendation** using the scale and the three perspectives in [04 §6](04-competencies-rubric-and-credit.md#6-credit-award-determination) [P1-A step 5; P1-B p.24, p.35].
- If they can't agree, the **Director** facilitates [P1-B p.24].

### Step 6: Writing the initial report (writer)

- Fill in the **template** [P1-F], using the **phrase inventory** where helpful [P1-B Appendix E p.104–106]. Sample reports are in Appendices Fi and Fii [P1-B p.107–117].
- **Template sections** [P1-F]:
  1. Header: student #, Discussion of Learning date, team (chair and writer), submission date
  2. Standard thank-you
  3. Program Plan: program name, credits available to PLAR (LL/UL)
  4. Award: LL and UL APST credits
  5. Assessment Summary: examples of progress, inconclusive areas, examples of competence, strengths
  6. Optional: course recommendations
  7. Overall Summary, with the I/S/O table by competency
- **Writing rules**:
  - positive and congratulatory
  - objective, with few "you" statements
  - specific examples from both the portfolio and the interview
  - defensible
  - unique to the student, "rather than a slave to the report format"

  [P1-B p.25, p.38–39]
- **Three audiences** [P1-B p.25–26]:
  1. The student
  2. **The audit trail.** This is "the only permanent record of the assessment, award, and reasons", so an independent third party must be able to follow it.
  3. Future programs and institutions
- Draft it soon after the interview [P1-B p.26].

### Step 7: Chair review

The chair reviews the draft **with track changes**, adds examples, and checks that the report is **specific and congruent**, meaning the award matches the narrative. It goes back to the writer to finalise. If the assessors can't agree, the Director adjudicates [P1-B p.26 "Step 7"; P1-A step 6].

### Step 8: Submission

- The report is emailed **to PLAR@tru.ca within one week of the interview** [P1-B p.13, p.26].
- The email should mention any potential for other PLAR streams (course challenges) [P1-B p.39].
- From there it goes into the decision process ([05 Stage 7](05-end-to-end-workflow.md#stage-7-decision-notification-credit-entry)).

## A.3 Information required and outcomes recorded at each step

| Step | Inputs needed | Outcomes that must be recorded | Who records |

| 0 Request | Assignment, portfolio files, Program Plan, any accommodation notice (P1-B p.41) | Acceptance; files accessible | Advisor / assessors |

| 3 Pre-meeting | The above | Roles; ice-breaker; final Chair and Writer question for each competency; reserve questions | Both (P1-C) |

| 8 Submit | Final report | Submission date; notes on other PLAR potential | Writer / chair → advisor logs it |
