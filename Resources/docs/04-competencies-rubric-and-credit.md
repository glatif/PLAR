# Competencies, Rubric, Rating and Credit Award

How learning is judged today. This matters for any AI that supports pre-assessment or scoring.

[← Back to index](index.md)

## 1. The eight Critical Competencies

They are based on the TRU Graduate Attributes. The current version is "Critical Competencies 2.0", introduced in 2020 [P1-B p.4–5 "Historical Context"].

| # | Competency | Short description given to students | Extra guidance for assessors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Communication Abilities | Communicating clearly and effectively in different modes and situations. The portfolio and the interview are themselves evidence. | Lower level: mastering situations. Upper level: developed principles and models. |
| 2 | Teamwork and Leadership Skills | Working with others to advance the group's work; self-awareness of one's own leadership style. | Lower level: understanding roles and what worked. Upper level: a situational approach. |
| 3 | Information Gathering and Organization | Gathering, sorting, synthesising and presenting information. **How the portfolio is organised is evidence.** | Research and analysis used as input to action. |
| 4 | Problem Solving and Decision-Making | Playing a problem-solving or decision role; sorting options and approaches. | Upper level: ethical dimensions, and articulated models. |
| 5 | Numeracy Skills | Working with numbers. | **The expected level depends on the program** (e.g. a BGS graduate vs a Health Science graduate). |
| 6 | Critical and Creative Thinking Abilities | Reason, logic, imagination, innovation; separating fact from value. | Upper level: weighing evidence, handling ambiguity, synthesis. |
| 7 | Independent Learning and Intellectual Maturity | Self-directed learning; personal growth; openness to others' views. | Openness to the needs of a pluralistic society. |
| 8 | Applied Knowledge and Skills | Specialised, transferable expertise applied at work or in the community. | Expertise that is transferable across situations. |

Sources: [P2-H p.5 "Eight Critical Competencies"; P2-E per-competency sections; P1-B p.15–16 "Description in Student Handbook / More Information for Assessors"].

- **The competencies overlap.** Evidence for one often shows up in another, and assessors are expected to recognise this [P1-B p.17 "Figure 7 Competency Connectivity"; P1-C "Introduction"].
- A weakness in communication **can unfairly lower the ratings of other competencies**. Assessors must guard against this [P1-B p.18, p.41–42].

## 2. Values and Commitments (looked for, not rated)

The four Values and Commitments in the Graduate Attributes are:

1. Social Responsibility & Ethical Behaviour
2. Sustainability & Entrepreneurship
3. Intercultural Understanding
4. Lifelong Learning

They are woven through the competencies rather than rated separately. What is looked for is the student's **thinking about** these values, not the values they hold [P1-C "Introduction"; P1-B p.5–6].

## 3. The rubric and its worksheet

- **Source of truth:** the "PLAR Competency Review Assessment Worksheet", updated July 2025 [P1-C]. Students and advisors use the same criteria as "Details of Competencies" [P1-I = P2-F].
- **Header fields** [P1-C]:
  - Student name, student number, date
  - Assessors (chair and writer)
  - Credits requested (LL/UL)
  - Credits recommended (LL/UL)
  - Ice-breaker question
- **For each of the 8 competencies** [P1-C]:
  - **Criteria for Lower-Level Credit**, plus "As demonstrated by, but not limited to" examples
  - **Criteria for Upper-Level Credit**, plus examples
  - Checkbox columns for **Chair** and **Writer**: yellow means the criterion is partly supported, green means fully supported
  - A free-text "Portfolio Evidence (observations)" column
  - **Chair Preliminary Ratings** (LL, UL) and **Writer Preliminary Ratings** (LL, UL)
  - **DoL Chair Question** and **DoL Writer Question** (DoL here means the interview)
  - **DoL Notes and Conclusions**
  - **Consensus Final Ratings** (LL, UL)
- **How assessors use it:**
  - Some type notes straight into the rubric; others take notes by hand [P1-B p.20–21].
  - A common technique: highlight criteria in green (clearly shown, with where in the portfolio) or yellow (implied). **Yellow criteria become interview questions** [P1-B p.34 "TIP"].
  - The "Criteria" lists are **not ranked and not exhaustive**. The "As demonstrated by" examples are illustrations only, and assessors often add new examples to the rubric [P1-B p.32–33].

## 4. Levels of learning (Bloom's and Webb's)

- **Bloom's Taxonomy** has six levels: Remember, Understand, Apply (lower-level thinking) and Analyze, Evaluate, Create (upper-level thinking). The sources include example learning statements for each [P1-H; P2-G].
- **Webb's Depth of Knowledge** is also used [P1-B p.14; P1-C "Introduction"].
- Assessor indicators [P1-B p.33]:
  - **Lower level:** handles situations positively; principles beginning to develop; aware that alternative approaches are needed; Bloom's remember / understand / apply.
  - **Upper level:** has models and principles; contingency ("if, then") thinking; matches strategies to situations; metacognition; Bloom's apply / analyse / evaluate.
- **Benchmark:** lower-level credit = the competencies of a two-year-program graduate; upper-level credit = a four-year-program graduate [P1-B p.18].

## 5. Rating rules (I / S / O)

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| **S** = meets several of the criteria. **O** = meets several, especially at a high level. **I** = the assessment did not substantiate the criteria. **I does not mean the student lacks the competency.** | P1-B p.32 "Rating the Competencies" |
| Each competency is rated **at least twice**: a preliminary rating after portfolio review, and a final rating after the interview, which goes to the Director as a recommendation. | P1-B p.31 |
| **O and S count the same** towards credit. | P1-B p.20 |
| Ratings are **holistic**: one criterion met to a very high degree, or several met minimally, can both earn S. "If it was as easy as applying a purely quantitative element, it could be machine marked." | P1-B p.33 |
| Assessors rate the portfolio **conservatively**. The interview can raise a rating; lowering it is rare. Reasons to lower: the portfolio overstated the learning, probing showed less depth, or the student made discriminatory statements. | P1-B p.21–23 |
| The strength of evidence can decide an I/S borderline case. | P1-B p.31 |
| **No double dipping**: learning that has already earned credit cannot earn credit again. Graduate credits are never used, except as AR history. | P1-B p.32; P2-I p.13–14 |
| A student does not need S in every competency; students can graduate with a C− average. | P1-B p.18; P2-I p.68 |
| Personal beliefs are not assessed, only the reasoning behind them (e.g. the vaccination example). | P1-B p.11–12, p.39–40 |
| Disagreements are resolved by discussion against the rubric, then escalated to the Director. | P1-B p.19, p.24 |

## 6. Credit award determination

- **Upper bound:** the APST room in the student's Program Plan, set by Program Advisors [P1-B p.35].
- **Assessment Scale** [P1-G]:

| Competencies rated S or O | Award |
|---|---|
| 0 | No credit recommended |
| 1–2 | Minimum: 10–30% of available credit |
| 3–4 | Partial: 30–60% |
| 5–6 | Partial: 60–90% |
| 7–8 | Full: 90–100% |

- **Three perspectives** the assessors apply [P1-B p.35 "Final Credit Award Determination"]:
  1. **Proportion:** e.g. 5 of 8 competencies rated S or O, with 15 credits available, gives about 9 credits.
  2. **Readiness:** is the student ready for the next level? For upper-level credit, is the student ready to graduate?
  3. **Assessor discretion:** a student who integrates their competencies may deserve full credit, while another student with the same count of siloed competencies may not.
- **Post-interview shortcut** [P1-B p.24 "Step 5"]:
  - All competencies S or O: full award.
  - S in 2–3 competencies: a case for at least 6 credits.
  - Below that: discuss with the Director.
- **Screening threshold:** students are accepted only if they are likely to earn **at least six APST credits** [P1-B p.7].
- **Ambiguities to note:**
  - The scale's percentage ranges overlap at the boundaries.
  - The "S in 2–3 means at least 6 credits" rule does not map cleanly onto the scale.
  - Together these mean the award is **guided by the scale but finally decided by judgement**. That shapes how any calculation should be presented. See [Q-ASSESS-4](stakeholder-questions.md#q-assess-4).

## 7. How results are recorded

The final report has a table with one row per competency and **LL and UL columns, each holding I, S or O**, plus the credits awarded at each level [P1-F "Overall Summary"; P1-SPR]. The sample report recorded upper-level ratings only: Satisfactory or Outstanding in all competencies except Numeracy [P1-SPR "Assessment Summary"].
