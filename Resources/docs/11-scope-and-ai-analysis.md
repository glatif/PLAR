## 1. Priority and phasing: assessment, interviews and coaching

### What the sources state

- Two projects: "**1. Assessment of Competency-based Portfolios (CBP)**" is "our top priority". "**2. PLAR Advisor feedback on Competency-based Portfolios**" is "our next priority" [EMAIL].
- Project #1 is described by its materials. They cover **the whole assessor process, interview included**:
  - "Steps Assessors go through for each Competency-based Portfolio assessment ... from the time they've been requested to do an assessment to submission of report/credit award recommendation"
  - the Question Bank, "used to help generate questions for **Discussion of Learning/interview**"
  - the **Interview Script**, "a tool that can assist assessors during their interview"
  - the **Mock Discussion of Learning video**

  [EMAIL]
- In the assessors' own process, the interview is step 4 of 7 [P1-A]. Its purposes include validating authenticity and adding verbal evidence [P1-B p.23].
- Project #2 covers "Steps the PLAR Advisors work through, **from student payment through to the portfolio being ready for assessment**" [EMAIL].

### What this implies

- **The interview is part of Project #1.** Nothing in the sources separates "portfolio assessment" from "interviews" into different phases. In TRU's vocabulary, "assessment" means the entire assessor process: pre-assessment, the Discussion of Learning, consensus and the report [P1-A; P1-B p.19–26].
- "Top priority / next priority" is an **order of importance**. The email does **not** say whether the projects should be built one after the other or in parallel, or whether one phase must finish before the next starts.
- In the student journey, coaching (Project #2) happens **before** assessment (Project #1). The stakeholders still ranked assessment first. One plausible reason is that assessor time is the scarcer resource, but **that reason is not stated**.

### What we are assuming / open

| Assumption | Status | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 = Project #1, including the interview workflow | Matches the email's grouping. Phasing itself not confirmed. | [Q-SCOPE-1](stakeholder-questions.md#q-scope-1) |
| Phase 2 = Project #2 (coaching), built after Phase 1 | Inferred from "next priority". Not confirmed. | [Q-SCOPE-1](stakeholder-questions.md#q-scope-1) |
| A Phase 1 assessment tool can work without the Project #2 student upload flow (portfolios imported from Moodle or email) | Assumption | [Q-SCOPE-3](stakeholder-questions.md#q-scope-3) |

## 2. What "portfolio assessment" means, and whether AI should score

### The project lead's current understanding

> "Portfolio assessment involves using AI to evaluate and score the student's portfolio... With LLMs, we could potentially analyze the portfolio content and generate structured assessments and scores."

### What the sources state

- The stakeholders want "**PLAR efficiencies using AI/technology**" for the two projects [EMAIL]. **They do not say which tasks AI should do.** Scoring, grading and automated rating are **not mentioned anywhere**.
- The assessment **is already structured**: a rubric with lower- and upper-level criteria, I/S/O ratings for each competency at each level, and a scale that converts the number of S/O ratings into a credit range [P1-C; P1-G; P1-B p.31–36]. So the premise that it "was not quantified in a consistent way" is only partly true. The **inputs** are structured; the **judgement** is deliberately holistic.
- The handbook argues against mechanical scoring: "If it was as easy as applying a purely quantitative element, it could be machine marked" [P1-B p.33]. The award also depends on readiness and discretion [P1-B p.35].
- Policy ED-2.0 gives the credit decision to faculty with subject expertise [P1-B p.3].
- The two-assessor model depends on **independent** preliminary ratings [P1-B p.22–23].

### What this implies

- **Efficiency through AI in assessment is clearly wanted.** **Automatic scoring is not established**, and it would conflict with several stated principles if it replaced human judgement. See [10 §2–3](10-principles-and-constraints.md).
- The options form a spectrum. Stakeholders should choose:

| Level | Description | Fit with the sources |
|---|---|---|
| A. Tooling only | Digital rubric, workspace, report template; no AI | Fully compatible |
| B. **AI as evidence mapper** | Maps portfolio passages to rubric criteria with citations; flags experience-only statements, gaps and double dipping; suggests questions; drafts the report | Compatible if assessors stay the decision-makers |
| C. AI suggested ratings | Proposes I/S/O ratings with justifications, which assessors accept or override | Risk of anchoring (P1-B p.22–23) and bias (P1-B p.39–40). Needs policy approval. |
| D. AI decides | AI produces the rating or award | Conflicts with ED-2.0 as cited (P1-B p.3) |

- **Our recommendation for the stakeholder discussion:** present Level B as the baseline and Level C as an option. Level D is out of scope unless policy says otherwise.

Questions: [Q-SCOPE-2](stakeholder-questions.md#q-scope-2), [Q-AI-1](stakeholder-questions.md#q-ai-1), [Q-AI-2](stakeholder-questions.md#q-ai-2).

## 3. AI-powered multimodal interviews

### What we were told (from outside the sources)

The project lead was told to build a component where a **multimodal LLM conducts a real-time interview** with the student. It would ask pre-prepared questions in a structured way, **record the whole video**, and then **analyse the recording to generate an assessment**.

### What the sources state

- **No source mentions AI interviews, recording, transcription or video analysis.** A text search of all the documents found no reference to AI, recording or transcripts in connection with the interview.
- The interview, as documented:
  - It is conducted by **two human assessors** (current or former TRU faculty or subject experts) [P1-B p.11, p.23; P2-I p.71].
  - It is "in essence, an oral examination", "similar to an oral thesis defence" [P2-I p.65; P1-B p.24].
  - It is held by Zoom or phone, and students may choose **audio only** [P2-I p.67].
  - Its purposes include checking authenticity and integrity, live probing, giving the student a chance for verbal evidence, and supporting oral communicators (P1-B p.23).
  - The questions are customised by the assessors after they have read the portfolio. Answers are acknowledged neutrally. Probing must not lead the student. Distress is handled by the assessors (P1-B p.36–38, p.41).
- Students are told the interview will be with their assessors. The PLAR Advisors "will not be present" [P2-I p.67].

### Where it would fit, if it is wanted

- It would belong to **Project #1 (Assessment)**, specifically **step 4 (Discussion of Learning)**, and would feed **step 5 (consensus)** [P1-A].
- It is **not** part of Project #2 (coaching).
- **One variant does relate to coaching:** an AI **mock interview** for practice. The sources already provide a mock Discussion of Learning **video** as a student resource [P1-J; EMAIL], and students are encouraged to prepare and practise questions [P2-I p.65]. A practice tool would support that without changing how assessment works.

### Possible variants to put to the stakeholders

| Variant | Description | Main tension with the sources |
|---|---|---|
| I-1 AI practice interview | Students rehearse with an AI interviewer before the real Discussion of Learning. Not assessed. | Low. Must not over-coach, or hint at "right answers" (P1-B p.37–38). |
| I-2 AI assistant in a human interview | Live transcription, a competency tracker, prompts to the chair, auto-filled notes | Consent and privacy. Audio-only option. |
| I-3 AI asks the questions; human assessors review the recording | Replaces the live panel with an asynchronous recorded interview | Loses live probing and the authenticity check. Cultural safety (P1-B p.26–27). Changes what students are promised (P2-I p.67). |
| I-4 Fully AI interview plus AI assessment | As described to the project lead | All of the above, plus §2 Level D concerns |

Questions: [Q-INT-1 … Q-INT-8](stakeholder-questions.md#2-ai-powered-multimodal-interviews). This is **not established** by the sources, so the stakeholders must confirm it before we design it.

### Project-team direction (updated 2026-10-22, project lead)

Whichever AI variant is chosen:

- **We will propose our own built-in interview room** as the preferred way to run and stream the Discussion of Learning, with **Zoom as a fallback** ([15 §2](15-technical-approach.md#2-interview-platform-our-own-room-preferred-with-zoom-as-a-fallback); Q-INT-7). Today interviews run on Zoom or teleconference, which an advisor connects (P2-I p.67).
- **We will propose photo-ID verification** before the interview. The student shows ID to the camera, a multimodal model **assists**, and **a person confirms**. Images are deleted promptly ([13 §6](13-features.md#how-identity-verification-would-work); [15 §4](15-technical-approach.md#4-identity-verification-technical-design); Q-INT-6, Q-INT-8, Q-PRIV-6). This extends today's verbal identity check by the chair (P1-E) and the interview's authenticity purpose (P1-B p.23, p.41).
- **The AI provider is not decided.** The options are the Microsoft Copilot ecosystem or a proprietary multimodal model through a protected endpoint. Any option must guarantee **no training on our data** ([15 §3](15-technical-approach.md#3-llm-integration-provider-options-and-privacy-requirements); Q-AI-7).

## 4. The 48-question readiness questionnaire

### What the sources state

- "48 yes/no questions that directly align with the eight competencies at the lower- and upper-level" [P1-B p.7].
- It is offered as **a link to a self-assessment questionnaire**, which replaced the "Knowledge Resume" process [P1-B p.8].
- It is an "**online questionnaire**", access-protected (the credential is deliberately not recorded here). "Advisors have created an **auto response** that lets students know the strength of their candidacy to continue **but it hasn't yet been activated**" [P2-A step 2].
- The student "complete[s] the PLAR Readiness Questionnaire and the PLAR Department will contact you with their recommendations" [P2-I p.10].
- The outcome feeds the advisor's decision along with the AR and the Program Plan [P1-B p.8; P2-A step 3].

### What is not stated

- **Which platform hosts it.** The sources do not say Moodle, Microsoft Forms, Qualtrics or anything else. Moodle access is only given **after** payment [P2-A step 5], which suggests the questionnaire is **not** inside the PLAR Moodle site. That is an inference.
- The 48 questions themselves (not provided), the scoring rule, and how results reach advisors.
- Whether the stakeholders want the questionnaire moved into the new platform.

### Status

- Rebuilding the questionnaire in our platform (a public link, responses stored, results visible to advisors, the auto-response turned on) is a **proposed feature**, not a requirement.
- **The one documented gap** is that an auto-response exists but has not been switched on. That is a clear efficiency opportunity, but its activation also needs approval.

Questions: [Q-QUEST-1 … Q-QUEST-4](stakeholder-questions.md#4-readiness-questionnaire).

## 5. Document collection and management

### What the sources state

Documents arrive by email, Moodle, website or mail/USB. Advisors comment in Word. Files are downloaded from and re-uploaded to Moodle. Assessors download from Moodle [P2-I p.62–63; P2-A step 7; P1-B p.19]. Full detail is in [08 §4](08-communications-scheduling-records.md#4-document-collection-review-and-communication).

### What is not stated

Whether the stakeholders want the platform to **replace** this (students upload, advisors review and comment, email notifications) or **supplement** it (keep Moodle and email, integrate selected parts). The phrase "needs to be downloaded from Moodle and then uploaded again" [P2-A step 7] hints at a pain point, but it is **not** a request.

Questions: [Q-DOC-1 … Q-DOC-4](stakeholder-questions.md#5-document-collection-review-and-communication).
