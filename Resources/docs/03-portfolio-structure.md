# Portfolio Structure and Submission Rules

What a student submits, the rules advisors check, and how files arrive today. These are the data objects the platform would need to model.

[← Back to index](index.md)

## 1. Components

The portfolio has seven components [P1-B p.13 "Portfolio Components"; P2-I table of contents; P2-B PDF p.15–16 "Moodle Access" email]. The stakeholders' email calls it "6 sections", which leaves out the evidence files themselves [EMAIL].

| # | Component | Purpose | Key rules advisors check | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cover Letter (CL)** | Introduces the student to the Director and assessors, says why they are pursuing PLAR, and states the credit request. | About 1 page, single-spaced, addressed and dated correctly, brief overview of learning categories, **accurate credit request**, includes T-ID. | P2-I p.21; P2-E "Cover Letter"; P1-B p.13 |
| 2 | **Table of Contents (ToC)** | Navigation. | 1–2 pages, single-spaced, all sections with page numbers, **lists evidence with file names**. | P2-I p.61; P2-E "Table of Contents"; P1-SP2 |
| 3 | **Autobiographical Resume (AR)** | Chronological overview of adult (post-high-school) experience. This is also the **screening document** used before the student enrols. | Clearly defined, dated sections in chronological order; acknowledges gaps; duration and full/part-time status of each role. Required headers: **Formal education (credits applied)**, **Informal education / credits not applied**, Employment, Hobbies/Interests, Volunteer. Cross-checked against the Program Plan. | P2-E "Autobiographical Resume"; P2-I p.25; P1-B p.44; P2-B PDF p.7 |
| 4 | **Identification of Learning (IoL)** | Broad "categories of learning" that give the DoL its skeleton. | Each category has 4 sub-headings: (1) where and how learning took place, (2) date and duration, (3) major topics, (4) supporting evidence. Usually 3–5 categories. A category must **not** be one of the 8 competencies and must **not** be a venue (e.g. "volunteering"). | P2-E "Identification of Learning"; P2-I p.29; P2-N; P1-SP4 |
| 5 | **Demonstration of Learning (DoL)** | The core section: one write-up for each of the 8 competencies. The success of the petition "will depend greatly on this section". | All 8 competencies covered; about 3–4 pages each (5 maximum), double-spaced, 12 pt, numbered pages, first person. Evidence referenced **within the narrative and listed at the end of each competency**. Learning, not experience. No credited formal education ("double dipping"). Only post-high-school learning, except brief references. | P2-I p.33–40; P2-E "Demonstration of Learning"; P2-B PDF p.4, p.10; P2-D "Only state learning..." |
| 6 | **Evidence Description and Listing (EDL)** | A caption for each piece of evidence explaining what it is and **why** it is included. | The 5 Ws (Who, What, Where, When, Why). **"Why" is mandatory** in every caption. 1–3 sentences each. Numbering is consistent with the evidence files. | P2-I p.57; P2-E "EDL"; P1-B p.13–14; P1-SP6 |
| 7 | **Evidence (EV)** | Documents that support the learning claims. | 16–24 pieces, **maximum 30** (about 2–3 per competency). Both internal and external evidence; **at least one reference letter**, verified by the advisor ("Verified by ___ on [DATE]. [Initials]"). Letterhead and signatures, authorship shown, limited photos, redaction weighed against validity. Numbered by competency (e.g. 1.1, 1.2), and one item may support several competencies. | P2-E "Evidence"; P1-B p.28–30, p.44–45; P2-K; P2-L; P2-B PDF p.4, p.8 |

Other required item: the **Statement of Authenticity and Confidentiality**, signed and emailed before portfolio work starts [P2-B PDF p.15; P2-I p.61 checklist].

Whole-portfolio checks: every page numbered, ToC page numbers correct, working hyperlinks, file-naming guidelines followed, university-standard writing [P2-I p.61 "Overall"]. Advisors also look for other PLAR potential (course-based, challenge exam, Credit Bank) and for double dipping in every section [P2-E header].

## 2. Evidence numbering model

The convention is `<competency number>.<sequence>`, e.g. `3.2 Vendor Management Practice` [P2-K; P1-SP2; P1-SP6].

- One piece of evidence can be listed under several competencies. For example, `5.2 Letter of Reference` appears under competencies 1, 3, 5 and 7 in the sample [P1-SP2].
- Evidence can be organised by competency or by IoL category [P2-K].
- A category with no evidence is marked "No Evidence Available" [P2-K].

## 3. How portfolios are submitted today

There are four submission methods [P2-I p.62–63 "Submitting Your Portfolio"]:

1. **Email** to PLAR@tru.ca. Students may send files in batches. Accepted formats: Microsoft documents, PDF, jpg, MP4, WMV. Advisors place the evidence into an "Evidence" folder.
2. **Website** built by the student.
3. **Mail.** Three printed copies, or a USB drive, with a shipping and handling fee.
4. **Moodle.** Described as a newer option, useful for large files.

Format rules:

- Drafts are sent as **Word documents**, because advisors comment directly in the files (especially the DoL).
- Final files may be PDF. Advisors convert Word files to PDF before they go to the assessors [P2-I p.62].
- Each section is a separate file named `YourNamePLARXXX` [P2-I p.62].
- Files over 20 MB need batching or Moodle [P2-I p.62].
- After the portfolio is approved for assessment, students may be asked to mail the complete version on a USB drive [P2-I p.62].

How advisors and assessors get the files:

- Advisors download sections from Moodle and upload them again after reviewing [P2-A step 7 "(needs to be downloaded from Moodle and then uploaded again)"].
- Assessors **download the portfolio from Moodle** [P1-B p.19 "Steps for Assessors"; P1-A step 2 "accesses portfolio on Moodle"].
- Administrative staff may handle "downloading and uploading student files" [P1-B p.11].
- Assessors also receive the student's **Program Plan** with the portfolio [P2-I p.14; P2-D "Only state learning..."].

What is not documented: who moves emailed files into Moodle, and whether all portfolios end up in Moodle. See [Q-DOC-2](stakeholder-questions.md#q-doc-2).

## 4. Writing guidance given to students (relevant to AI coaching design)

- **Experience → learning → application.** "What did I do? What did I learn? How did I apply that learning?", with the emphasis on the last two [P2-B PDF p.15].
- A worked example of turning a statement of experience into a statement of learning [P2-J; P2-I p.39].
- Five reflection questions per example: what was learned, how it was learned (including mistakes), the process followed, a specific story, and application elsewhere [P2-J p.2].
- **Bloom's Taxonomy** decides which examples to use. Upper-level petitions need analyse, evaluate and create examples [P1-H; P2-G; EMAIL; P2-B PDF p.4, p.10].
- Prompting questions for each competency [P2-M].
- A brainstorming method for choosing 4–5 examples and assigning them to competencies [P2-O].
- The **"two co-workers" test**: two people can have the same experience but not the same learning [P2-C comment KJ2; P2-D "Speak To the Learning"].
- **STARL** structure for interview answers [P1-B p.46].
